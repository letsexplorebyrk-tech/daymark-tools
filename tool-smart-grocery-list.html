How it works

Add an item and the tool files it into the right section - kitchen or home - so the list reads in the order you actually walk the shop. When you add something like atta or rice, it may suggest the items that commonly run out with it. You accept only the suggestions that help; nothing is added without your tap. Tick items off as you shop, clear the list when you are done.

Built for mixed-language kitchens

Most grocery apps choke the moment you type haldi instead of turmeric. This one treats English and Marathi as first-class citizens, because that is how real shopping lists look. Add items in whichever language comes to mind first and the sections still come out right.

Example: build a list that works in the shop

Add “tomato”, choose Produce, and add a quantity such as “4” with unit “pieces”. You can also type a brand or local item that is not in the suggestion catalogue; suggestions are optional, and your own text remains editable. Mark an item done as you shop, or remove it if plans change.

When suggestions may miss

Automatic sorting recognises common items, not every spelling, brand, or regional name. Check the assigned section and change it when needed. The English and Marathi catalogue is a convenience, not a translation service.

Private by design

Your list lives in your browser's local storage, on your device, and nowhere else. No account, no sync server, no one analysing what your household eats. It survives a closed tab; it never leaves your phone.
